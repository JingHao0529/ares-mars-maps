Sources and calculations

How we worked it out

We use Mars's size to work out distances. Inside the Jezero square, we use measured heights to check slopes. Going uphill costs more, so a longer route can sometimes be easier.

The travel speeds are estimates: 2.5 km/h on foot, 6 km/h for a rover and 4 km/h for a cargo rover. These are our settings, not official NASA vehicle speeds. We add extra time for climbing where heights are known.

We look at old dust-storm observations and prefer routes with less recorded activity. The records only cover selected times, so an empty area does not mean it is safe. This is not a live storm forecast.

Radiation builds up with time, so the planner gives some extra weight to shorter travel times. The dose shown is just a comparison using Curiosity's old Gale Crater measurement. It cannot tell us the real dose somewhere else or during a solar storm.

Phoenix found perchlorate in soil samples. We can show where it was found, but we do not know how far it spreads. The temperature and UV report is also from one place and date. We cannot use these as a complete danger map.

Outside the Jezero square, the route is still a rough illustration. It can compare past dust activity, but it cannot check hills or small obstacles. It should not be used to plan a real journey.

One sol is about 24 Earth hours and 40 minutes. The clock uses NASA's Mars24 equations and shows mean solar time at the starting point, or at 0° east if no start is set.

Daylight uses NASA’s Mars24 equations to work out where the Sun is. We count daylight when the centre of the Sun is above a flat horizon. Mountains, dust and shadows are not included. We sample travel in steps of up to five Earth minutes. If you pause at night, the total time includes waiting for sunrise. Near the poles, we only search three sols ahead for the next sunrise.

Stops add the number of Earth minutes you choose. Reordering stops changes the route. A stop is not a real charging station just because you put it on the map.

Battery use is distance divided by your full-charge range. The starting ranges are example settings: 100 km for the rover and 60 km for cargo. Cold, hills, equipment and battery ageing can change the real range. Selecting a full recharge assumes your stop has enough power and time to do it. The walking budget starts at 20 km for the whole trip; it does not predict oxygen or suit limits.
