Sources and methods

Surface imagery and measured heights come from real NASA and USGS data. The optional warm color tint is a display effect, not a true-color observation. Routes and slopes are calculations made by ARES.

Illustrative routes outside Jezero

Outside the measured area, route lines use randomized waypoints. They are illustrations, not routes inferred from NASA imagery or terrain. They do not assess hazards, slope or traversability. Distances describe the drawn line; travel times use illustrative transport speeds.

How measurements work

Coordinates use Mars planetocentric latitude and east-positive longitude. Distances use a Mars radius of 3,396,190 m. Elevations are relative to the MOLA areoid.

Jezero elevation is a 512 × 512 crop at 20.163 m projected spacing from a USGS CTX stereo DTM. Null cells remain unknown. Slope is a central-difference estimate with latitude-adjusted east–west spacing.

Basemaps and the DTM were processed independently and can have alignment offsets. THEMIS layers show historical infrared mosaics, not current temperature. Route cost = distance + uphill weight × cumulative ascent. A* chooses the lowest modeled cost on the terrain grid; a longer detour is preferred when it is cheaper. Downhill travel adds distance cost but no uphill penalty. Travel time and routing preferences are set by your selected transport profile. Presets are adjustable in the application source and are illustrative assumptions, not certified astronaut or vehicle performance limits. No radiation, mineral, weather or oxygen values are invented.
